How it plays

Four lanes, one arrow each, a receptor line near the bottom of the screen. Arrows scroll down in time with the song and you tap the lane the moment the arrow sits on its receptor. Hold notes have a tail: keep your thumb down until it ends. Jumps put two arrows on the same beat.

    Three quarters of a note. Still keeps the combo going.

  • Good within 150 ms

    Forty percent. A tap this loose still counts, barely.

  • Miss 180 ms late

    Nothing, and the combo resets. Letting go of a hold early breaks it the same way.

Score and grade

A run is worth up to 1,000,000 points, spread evenly over every note. A hold counts twice: once for the tap and once for keeping it. Your accuracy is the share of those points you earned, and it sets the grade.

Survival

The bar along the top is your health. It starts half full, climbs a little with every hit, and drops hard on a miss or a broken hold. Reach zero and the song fails. Survival is on by default; switch it off in Settings and a bad run simply ends with a low grade instead.

Three charts per song
Easy, Normal and Hard, each with its own level number. Harder charts add jumps, holds and off-beat steps, never faster music.
Note speed and offset
How fast the arrows fall is yours to set, from one to ten. If your speakers or headphones lag, an audio offset shifts every note to match, in five millisecond steps.

The music

Original, and royalty-free

Nothing in Dancy Fingers is sampled, licensed or covered. A composer program writes each piece, picking a key, a tempo, a chord progression and a verse, chorus and bridge, then spinning motif-based melodies over them. A synthesizer renders every note, chord and drum hit from raw waveforms. Song titles and artist names come out of word lists; they are not real songs and not real artists.

Because the charts are built from the exact musical events that produced the audio, every arrow lands on a note that is actually there. No hand-timing, no drift.

A composer in your pocket

The app carries the same composer and synthesizer, ported to run on the phone. Tap Generate Song on the main menu, and a fresh 70 to 90 second track with all three charts appears in the song list after fifteen to thirty seconds, marked ✦ Generated. Keep the ones you like, delete the rest. There is no limit.

Eight styles rotate through the built-in set: synthpop, chiptune, house, drum & bass, lo-fi, trance, funk and hardcore. One of each is playable on this page.

About

Where it came from

Dancy Fingers started with a simple problem: rhythm games live or die on their music, and music is the one thing a small studio cannot license forty times over. So the music got written by a program instead, and the charts fell out of the same program for free. The game around them is a classic four-lane dance game, shrunk to a phone and tuned for two thumbs.

The version on this page is that game, ported to run in a browser, with eight of the forty songs. Your scores and settings stay in this browser.

Who made it

Dancy Fingers is made by eiilo, a small independent studio. The app is free, supported by ads that appear between songs and never during one; the version on this page has no ads at all. No account and no sign-up: your scores, settings and generated songs stay on your phone. Sign in to Google Play Games, if you like, for leaderboards and achievements. The app also shares usage statistics, and what its ads need, with Google, with your consent where the law requires it. The privacy policy spells all of that out.

Found a bug, or want a style the composer does not do yet? Write to hi@eiilo.com.

Take all forty with you

The app has every song, the composer for unlimited new ones, leaderboards and achievements on Google Play Games, and a screen made for thumbs instead of a mouse.
